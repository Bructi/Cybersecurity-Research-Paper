# Appendix C: International AI Cybersecurity Regulatory & Governance Frameworks Comparison

**Project:** Cybersecurity in the Age of Artificial Intelligence  
**Document Reference:** RP-APP-C  
**Auditor:** Cyber-AI Research Consortium  
**Jurisdictions Evaluated:** European Union, United States, Five Eyes, International Standards Organization (ISO)  

---

### Comparative Governance Matrix

| Framework / Regulation | Governing Authority / Body | Enforceability & Status | Core Cybersecurity Mandates | Specific AI Risk Focus | Key Strengths | Primary Operational Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EU Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation 2024/1689)** | European Parliament & Council (EU) | Legally Binding across 27 EU States (Enacted 2024; staged enforcement through 2026–2027) | Mandatory cybersecurity risk management, logging, accuracy testing, and CE conformity assessments for High-Risk AI Systems; systemic risk audits for General Purpose AI (GPAI). | Adversarial robustness, data poisoning, cybersecurity resilience against prompt injection, deepfake transparency / watermarking. | World's first comprehensive binding legal framework; severe financial penalties (up to €35M or 7% global turnover). | High compliance burden on SMEs; technical standard definitions (CEN/CENELEC) still maturing. |
| **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (NIST AI 100-1 / AI 600-1)** | U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) | Voluntary Consensus Standard (Mandated across U.S. Federal Agencies via OMB directives) | Structured framework across four core functions: **Govern, Map, Measure, Manage**. NIST AI 600-1 adds 12 risk profiles for Generative AI. | Evasion attacks, data poisoning, privacy leakage, model extraction, and indirect prompt injection. | Highly actionable engineering guidelines; seamlessly maps into NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF 2.0). | Non-binding on private sector entities unless incorporated into federal procurement contracts. |
| **NIST CSF Profile for AI (NIST IR 8596 / Cyber AI Profile)** | NIST NCCoE & U.S. Department of Commerce | Voluntary Implementation Guidance (2025/2026) | Integrates AI engineering into CSF 2.0 functions (Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover). | Securing AI systems, leveraging AI for cyber defense, and thwarting AI-enabled adversarial threats. | Directly operationalizes AI defense for enterprise CISOs and SOC architectures. | Focuses on defensive implementation; does not provide formal algorithmic verification standards. |
| **CISA Agentic AI Guidance & Five Eyes Directives** | U.S. CISA, ACSC (Australia), CCCS (Canada), NCSC-UK, NCSC-NZ | Inter-Agency Technical Directives (May–June 2026) | Security-by-Design mandates for tool-calling agentic AI; privilege separation, human-in-the-loop gates for destructive commands. | Excessive agency, tool invocation hijacking, autonomous agent privilege escalation, memory poisoning. | Grounded in real-world intelligence telemetry from Five Eyes cyber defense agencies. | Guidance is advisory; relies on sector-specific regulatory adoption. |
| **ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (AI Management System - AIMS)** | International Organization for Standardization (ISO) / IEC | Certifiable International Standard | Establishes requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an AI Management System. | AI lifecycle risk assessment, data provenance, model traceability, third-party AI supplier governance. | Globally recognized certification standard facilitating cross-border commercial compliance. | Process-oriented; does not prescribe specific mathematical thresholds for adversarial robustness. |
| **EU NIS2 Directive & DORA** | European Union (NIS2 Directive 2022/2555; DORA Regulation 2022/2554) | Legally Binding on Critical Infrastructure & Financial Entities (Enforced 2024–2025) | Stringent 24-hour early incident notification; mandatory supply-chain risk auditing; ICT third-party provider oversight. | Resilience of automated AI-driven financial trading and SOC triage systems against operational disruption. | Legally enforces supply-chain liability and executive management accountability. | Covers cybersecurity broadly; does not contain AI-specific algorithmic evaluation protocols. |
